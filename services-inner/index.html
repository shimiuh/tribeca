Consultation and Design

The flooring of every space varies slightly from one to the next. During the initial consultation -- either by phone or at the job site -- we conduct an in-depth analysis of the flooring project, listen to your expectations, and explain all of the available options for achieving your goals.

What you can expect from your Tribeca Flooring team:
  1. Discuss your goals for your flooring solution
  2. Measure your flooring work area
  3. Examine your existing subfloor and wood flooring
  4. Assess the condition of your current flooring
  5. Discuss sound reduction, moisture protection, subfloor options, flooring options, and finish options
  6. Address your questions and concerns
  7. Discuss necessary repairs if applicable
  8. Provide a cost estimate

Supply of Materials

At Tribeca Flooring we use the following quality flooring products for installation. These materials are also available for purchase. We have the resources and expertise to meet any project scope or budget.

Prefinished Wood

Hardwood flooring that is sanded, stained, and finished in the factory 
Advantages:
  1. The factory-applied wood finish is very durable. The more coats applied, the more protected the floor is. Factories can apply up to 14 coats, resulting in a stronger finish, which protects the floor against wear and tear.
  2. These planks are manufactured with micro bevels—rounded edges to help the sharp corners of the wooden planks align perfectly. This method is used in place of sanding the floors, which would eliminate any alignment issues.
  3. Wood samples depict exactly how the completed flooring project will look.
  4. Repairs are easy, and replacement pieces of wood can be procured from the same collection to match flooring perfectly.
  5. Many more finishing options are available. Finishing plants are not as limited in their capabilities as job sites are, so the plants are able to manipulate a multitude of finishes in their controlled environment.
  6. No mess is left behind because no sanding is required.
  7. The overall project time is shorter.
  8. Because this wood is already finished, there is no need to move furniture out of your home while the floors are sanded and finished. We can move your furniture as needed.
  9. No lag time; the floor is ready to be walked upon the day it is installed.
  10. Prefinished wood can be re-sanded and refinished just like unfinished wood.
  11. There are less fumes with a pre-finished wood floor.
  12. There are better warranties with pre-finished floors.
Disadvantages
  1. The micro bevels around the edges of the wood planks collect dust and can be more challenging to clean than smooth floors. (There is one company that offers prefinished wood without micro bevels. This product can only be used if your subfloor is perfectly straight. Otherwise, sharp edges may result.)
  2. Complex patterns are more difficult to achieve with pre-finished wood.
Unfinished Wood
Traditional raw, hardwood flooring that is installed raw, and then sanded, stained, and finished on site.
Advantages:
  1. The sanding process creates a completely flat and smooth floor (no bevels, no grooves), resulting in a floor that appears as one whole piece of wood.
  2. Easy to clean because there are no grooves to trap dirt.
  3. Freedom to customize these floors to your heart’s desire—any grade, size, or species of wood, any color of stain, inlays, borders, medallions, patterns, and levels of sheen.
Disadvantages
  1. It is more difficult to repair unfinished wood because the finish was applied as one coat to the entire floor as a whole. In order for the floor to appear uniform, the whole floor must be refinished.
  2. Additional time is needed for sanding and finishing the floors.
  3. Each piece of unfinished wood is unique, so the completed floor will not match the flooring sample exactly. The stain sample does not take into account the various types of wood that you might have used throughout the flooring project area. Additionally, the stain sample does not take into account the polyurethane coats that will be applied on top of it.
  4. The sanding process produces a lot of dust. For an additional fee, we offer an effective dustless process that will remove the dust while the floors are being installed.
  5. Odor varies by finish type. Some finishes smell awful and some finishes hardly have any smell at all.
How Do You Choose?

Consider: Can you clear your entire jobsite for it to be sanded? Do you want a unique, custom floor with specific design and color? Do you mind bevels in your floor? Do you have the necessary time to finish the floors on-site?

The choice is yours. We will provide you with unmatched service, regardless of your choice of flooring. The final project cost depends on your unique needs and circumstances.

There are two types of wood structures, solid and engineered, which are both available in unfinished and factory-finished wood:
Solid wood
Structure:

Traditional hardwood flooring that is comprised of a solid wood plank, generally ¾ inches thick.

Availability:

These generally come in 2¼-inch-wide to 7-inch-wide planks and can be custom-made up to 12-inches wide.

Environment:

Wood naturally expands and contracts with moisture, so it is important to maintain a steady level of humidity in the house.

Application Method:

This flooring must be adhered to a wood subfloor using nail or staples. It is recommended to glue the floor down as well, making the floor even stronger. This holds the floor in place when moisture levels fluctuate in the home.

Benefit:

A great benefit of solid hardwood is that is can be sanded and refinished many, many times.

Engineered wood
Structure:

Flooring comprised of two products, solid wood veneer (wear) layer and engineered wood layer. The engineered layers of wood have grains that go in opposite directions from the grains of the solid wood that is above it, allowing for the layers to support one another and resist natural expanding and contracting.

Availability:

The engineered layer generally comes in ⅜-inch-thick to ¾-inch-thick planks. The veneer layer above it generally comes 1-millimeter-thick to 6-millimeter-thick.  Because it is a stronger product, wider planks are more common.

Environment:

While humidity levels in the environment cause a solid wood floor to expand and contract, engineered wood has the benefit of being more stable and more resistant to those changes in humidity levels. Although engineered wood is more stable than solid wood, no wood is completely resistant to changes in humidity levels in the environment.

Application Method:

This flooring must be adhered to a wood subfloor using nail or staples. It is recommended to glue the floor down as well, making the floor even stronger. This holds the floor in place when moisture levels fluctuate in the home.

Benefit:

A great benefit of solid hardwood is that is can be sanded and refinished many, many times.

How Do You Choose?

Consider your priorities: What kind of subfloor do you currently have? What solution is the best fit for your environment? How wide of a plank would you like in your flooring?

The following materials are used beneath the wood flooring as an underlay:
Subflooring wood

Base of the flooring laid on top of your existing concrete foundation. It is used to support the wood flooring to be laid above it, which can be nailed down into the subfloor. Manufactured as plywood or Advantech [link to website]. Alternatively, a joist slab of wood can also be used.

Subflooring wood is used for the following reasons:

  • To nail a wood floor to it (a wood subfloor is required to nail into)
  • To level the floor before installing any type of floor–applied with nails, glue, or by floating the floor above it.
Sound-reduction products

Underlayments to reduce traveling noise. There are multiple options for sound-reduction such as rubber underlayment, foam underlayment, or 3-in-one glue, which works as a moisture and sound reduction barrier in addition to the adhesive.

Carpet Removal

If your floors are covered with carpeting, it must be removed prior to installing the new wood flooring. We cut and lift all types of carpeting, tacked and glued down, as well as any padding underneath the carpeting. We also remove tack strips from around the walls and staples covering the floor. Environmentally-sensitive disposal and carpet recycling are available.

Please note: Pricing for the complete flooring project is not final until carpets are removed and the flooring beneath it evaluated.

Our crews are highly skilled woodworkers and are paid premium rates. Therefore, it is costly to pay them to lift your carpets ahead of installing wood flooring. To cut costs, you may remove the carpeting yourself.

Read our Step-By-Step Guide to Removing Carpeting

Removing carpet on your own is a big, messy job, but if you are trying to save a few bucks, this is a good option.
  1. Collect: a sharp retractable knife, duct tape, hammer, pry-bar, pliers, knee pads, and broom or handheld vacuum.
  2. Use a dust mask and sturdy gloves—these will prevent dirt kicked up from beneath the carpet from getting in your airway and save your hands from getting cut by protruding nails beneath the carpet as well.
  3. Cut the carpet into manageable strips, such as 4-feet wide and the length of the room.
  4. Pull the carpet strips away from the wall at one end of the room.
  5. Pile the strips on the last strip and roll it all up into a ball to discard.
  6. Do the same for the underlay, the cushion beneath the carpet.
  7. Remove all of the tack-strips from around the walls using a pry bar or a hammer and flathead screwdriver. Remove the larger nails first to loosen the tack strips.
  8. Break up the tack strips into manageable pieces, roll them all together and discard.
  9. Remove the staples and tiny nails around the perimeter of the room.
  10. Sweep or vacuum the area thoroughly.
  11. Do a final walk-through to remove any nails or staples that may have escaped your eyes the first time around.
  12. Put everything you are discarding in a dump or dispose of it in a more environmentally-conscious manner by bringing it to a local recycling location.

Plywood Subfloor Leveling and Installation

A quality floor begins with a quality subfloor as a foundation. There are three methods to choose from for installing subflooring: Sleepers, plywood-on-slab and floating subfloor system.

A note: For a floor that is not yet completely level, we place 6×6-inch boxes upon the base and then lay the subfloor on top. Finally, the wood floor is installed flush against the level subfloor.
Sleepers

The sleeper system is designed to prevent warping and mold growth, which ultimately extends the life of your floor. Sleepers are 2-by-4 lengths of wood on the slab spaced 16 inches on center.

Plywood

One layer of ¾ inch plywood or Advantech: The subfloor, comprised of plywood or Advantech, is installed directly onto the existing floor – either a concrete slab or wood joists – and attached with screws for wood subfloor or split drive anchors for concrete subfloor.

Floating subfloor

Double-layer of 4/8” floating plywood or Advantech. The two layers are laid out in opposite directions, and are then glued and screwed together. The subfloor can be floated over foam or rubber insulation. This is the best solution for applying subflooring on top of sound barrier material because it can be done without puncturing it with nails.

Plywood Subfloor Leveling and Installation

Installation methods vary and depend on multiple factors including type of subfloor location, the level of the house where it is being installed, and the structure of the hardwood being used.

Prefinished and Unfinished Wood
Nail-down only:

Each plank is directly nailed to the subfloor. This method of installation requires a ¾ inch plywood subfloor and is the required minimum for all solid wood flooring.

Adhesive only (no nails used):

Each plank is glued directly onto the subfloor. This installation is recommended only for engineered wood, not solid wood, because engineered wood is more stable than solid wood. There are adhesive manufacturers who say you can glue down solid. However, we have seen this method fail and therefore we do not recommend it.

Combination nail-down and adhesive:

Wood is responsive to the climate and weather; it expands in humidity and contracts in dry heat. The wider the wood planks, the stronger this effect is. Glue can be used in installation as an additional measure to hold the floor in place when the wood expands and contracts during the change of seasons and subsequent humidity levels.

Floating:

Planks of flooring are attached by interlocking the tongues and grooves of the wood, or glued to each other, but are not attached directly to the subfloor. As the floor contracts and expands with the temperature and humidity, it moves as one unit. This application is only used for engineered wood because engineered wood is more stable.
