Lesson 2

Materials and finish systems

The material decision controls installation, schedule, durability, repair strategy, and final look.

PlanningMaterialsSite preparationSubfloor and soundInstallationSandingStainingRepairsMaintenanceFull project pathOriginal lesson sourceVisual library

Prefinished wood

Prefinished hardwood is sanded, stained, and finished in the factory. Factory finishes can be very durable because controlled plants can apply many coats and produce consistent color.

Use prefinished material when the schedule is tight, fumes and sanding dust need to be minimized, furniture cannot be fully moved out, or the client wants to see exactly how the finished product will look from samples.

  • Advantages: durable factory finish, faster project time, easier matching repairs, more factory finish options, less mess, and immediate walkability.
  • Tradeoffs: micro bevels collect dust, complex patterns are harder, and the subfloor must be straight enough to avoid sharp edges or alignment issues.

Unfinished wood

Unfinished wood is installed raw, then sanded, stained, and finished on site. This creates a flat, smooth floor with no bevels or grooves, so the floor can read as one continuous wood surface.

Use unfinished material when the project needs custom color, a completely smooth plane, complex patterns, or site-specific stain sampling.

Solid wood

Solid wood requires a more conservative installation plan. Nail-down installation over a proper plywood subfloor is the baseline because solid wood moves strongly with humidity.

Engineered wood

Engineered wood is more dimensionally stable. It can support glue-down and floating methods that are not recommended for solid wood in many job conditions.

Teaching point

The question is not "Which floor is best?" The correct question is "Which floor fits this room, this subfloor, this schedule, this finish expectation, and this budget?"
