Lesson 4

Subfloor and sound control

A quality floor begins with a quality subfloor. Level, stable, dry, and quiet comes before beautiful.

Three subfloor systems

Sleepers

Sleepers are 2-by-4 lengths of wood on a slab, commonly spaced 16 inches on center. The sleeper system helps prevent warping and mold growth by creating a stable foundation.

Plywood or Advantech

One layer of 3/4 inch plywood or Advantech can be installed directly onto concrete or wood joists and attached with screws or anchors. This creates the required base for many nail-down installations.

Floating subfloor

A double layer of floating plywood or Advantech is laid in opposite directions, then glued and screwed together. It can float over foam or rubber insulation, making it useful when sound barrier material should not be punctured.

Leveling sequence

  1. Map high and low spots before bringing in finish material.
  2. Build up low spots with custom boxes or other approved leveling assemblies.
  3. Lay the plywood or subfloor assembly on top.
  4. Confirm the result is level, stable, and appropriate for the chosen installation method.

Sound reduction choices

Foam underlayment

Used under floating floors. It is not glued or nailed because the foam can tear.

Rubber underlayment

Made from recycled material, useful between concrete and plywood or concrete and engineered wood. It can support glued, floated, or some anchored assemblies.

Three-in-one adhesive

Acts as adhesive while also reducing sound and moisture movement in suitable glue-down installations.

Floating over rubber

A double plywood layer can float over rubber so anchors do not puncture the sound barrier.
