Lesson 1

Planning and design

Every floor varies slightly. The right consultation turns a vague flooring idea into a buildable scope, budget, and sequence.

Start with the job, not the product

The original Tribeca process begins with an in-depth analysis of the flooring project. Listen to the client's expectations, inspect the existing floor and subfloor, then explain the available options for reaching the goal.

Teach the learner to ask why the floor is being changed: appearance, durability, sound reduction, moisture protection, repair, resale, or a construction schedule. The answer changes the material, installation method, finish, and budget.

What to inspect

  • Measure the work area and identify transitions, closets, stairs, door clearances, radiators, and fixed millwork.
  • Examine the existing subfloor and wood flooring before promising a final price.
  • Assess the condition of current flooring, including water damage, loose boards, previous repairs, and uneven areas.
  • Discuss sound reduction, moisture protection, subfloor options, flooring options, and finish options.
  • Address questions, concerns, necessary repairs, and jobsite constraints before work starts.

Approval points

  1. Confirm scope, rooms, and square footage.
  2. Confirm subfloor strategy and whether leveling or sound control is required.
  3. Confirm wood type, grade, width, pattern, and finish direction.
  4. Confirm schedule, furniture handling, dust protection, access, and cleanup expectations.

Field rule

Do not treat a wood floor as a surface-only job. A beautiful finish on top of a bad subfloor, moisture issue, or wrong installation method will still fail.
