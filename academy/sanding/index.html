Lesson 6

Sanding and refinishing

Sanding removes finish carefully, exposes raw wood, and prepares the floor for color and protection.

PlanningMaterialsSite preparationSubfloor and soundInstallationSandingStainingRepairsMaintenanceFull project pathOriginal lesson sourceVisual library

The purpose of sanding

The sanding process uses progressively finer abrasive material to remove layers of finish covering the hardwood. When raw wood is exposed, it can be stained with color, polished, or finished natural.

Use dustless sanding equipment where appropriate. It uses specialized machinery with vacuum suction to collect dust and sand, but sealing off areas is still part of a professional setup.

Refinishing sequence

  1. Seal off areas to protect against dust when needed.
  2. Set up dustless equipment and clean the floor before sanding.
  3. Inspect for protruding splinters, nails, or loose boards.
  4. Sand with the proper machines, including edgers where the large machine cannot reach.
  5. Vacuum, inspect, and sand again with progressively finer abrasive.
  6. Prepare for stain, seal, and finish coats.

Finish protection

Varnish preserves wood, protects it from scratches, and highlights natural beauty. Common systems include oil-based polyurethane, water-based polyurethane, natural oil, and hardwax oil.
