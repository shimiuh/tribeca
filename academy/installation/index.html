Lesson 5

Installation methods

Installation depends on subfloor, house level, humidity behavior, plank width, and whether the flooring is solid or engineered.

Choose the method

Nail-down only

Each plank is nailed directly to the subfloor. This requires a 3/4 inch plywood subfloor and is the required minimum for solid wood flooring.

Adhesive only

Each plank is glued directly to the subfloor. This is recommended for engineered wood, not solid wood, because engineered wood is more stable. Solid glue-down can fail when wood movement is too strong.

Combination nail-down and adhesive

Wood expands in humidity and contracts in dry heat. Wider planks show this more strongly. Glue can be added as a stabilizing measure when seasonal movement is a concern.

Floating

Planks are attached to each other by locking tongues and grooves or glue, but are not attached to the subfloor. The floor moves as one unit. This is used for engineered wood because engineered wood is more stable.

Do not ignore movement

Wood responds to climate and weather. A method that looks easier on day one can become the wrong method after seasonal humidity changes.

Installation workflow

  1. Confirm wood acclimation and jobsite conditions.
  2. Confirm subfloor flatness, fastener schedule, and moisture readings.
  3. Rack boards for color, length, and pattern before fastening or gluing.
  4. Maintain expansion gaps and clean adhesive as work progresses.
  5. Inspect transitions, borders, stairs, and wall lines before final cleanup.
