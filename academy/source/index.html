Carpet Removal

If your floors are covered with carpeting, it must be removed prior to installing the new wood flooring.

We cut and lift all types of carpeting, tacked and glued down, as well as any padding underneath the carpeting. We also remove tack strips from around the walls and staples covering the floor. Environmentally-sensitive disposal and carpet recycling are available.

note : Pricing for the complete flooriPleaseng project is not final until carpets are removed and the flooring beneath it evaluated.

Our crews are highly skilled woodworkers and are paid premium rates. Therefore, it is costly to pay them to lift your carpets ahead of installing wood flooring. To cut costs, you may remove the carpeting yourself.

STEP-BY-STEP GUIDE TO REMOVING CARPETING

Removing carpet on your own is a big, messy job, but if you are trying to save a few bucks, this is a good option.

Collect: a sharp retractable knife, duct tape, hammer, pry-bar, pliers, knee pads, and broom or handheld vacuum.

Use a dust mask and sturdy gloves - these will prevent dirt kicked up from beneath the carpet from getting in your airway and save your hands from getting cut by protruding nails beneath the carpet as well.

Cut the carpet into manageable strips, such as 4-feet wide and the length of the room.

Pull the carpet strips away from the wall at one end of the room.

Pile the strips on the last strip and roll it all up into a ball to discard.

Do the same for the underlay, the cushion beneath the carpet.

Remove all of the tack-strips from around the walls using a pry bar or a hammer and flathead screwdriver. Remove the larger nails first to loosen the tack strips.

Break up the tack strips into manageable pieces, roll them all together and discard.

Remove the staples and tiny nails around the perimeter of the room.

Sweep or vacuum the area thoroughly.

Do a final walk-through to remove any nails or staples that may have escaped your eyes the first time around.

Put everything you are discarding in a dump or dispose of it in a more environmentally-conscious manner by bringing it to a local recycling location.

How many days will it take to restore my hardwood floors?

November 1, 2016

Most average sized houses in Naperville have a living room, dining room, hallway, entrance and a couple of bedrooms. If the hardwood floors are in good shape and don't need any repairs and you're going to keep them natural and not stain them, you're looking at an average of 3 days. Bigger and more complicated layouts will obviously take a little longer.

One the first day we remove all the existing finish and prep the floor for the first sealer coat. Depending on the size of your home, we can usually apply this first coat by the end of the day. This is the longest day. Over the next 2 days the remaining coats are applied.

If you would like to have your floors stained, be prepared to add at least another day to the process. Other extras like restoring stairs, removing carpet and fixing any repairs that are needed will also add extra time to the job.

Will the smell and fumes be really bad?

Many people are worried about this. You may have smelled the rotten fumes from solvent based finishes in the past. Even worse are the fumes from conversion varnish systems (like Glitsa Swedish finishes) which can stink and gas off for weeks, which makes it very uncomfortable to stay at home during this time. We decided to stop using these nasty smelling and dangerous finishes a long time ago. Now we only specialize in using high-end water based finish systems which have an extremely low non-offensive odor for a short period of time as they dry. As they dry they release water vapor instead of harmful solvents which can damage the health of your family and pets. This makes it much easier for you if you need to stay in a section of your home during the restoration process as you won't have to worry about being driven out by eye watering fumes and off-gassing.

Can I just clean and put a new coat of finish my floors?

You may have seen some of these new businesses coming out of the woodwork promising amazing results for your floors by just using a "newly discovered" recoating system that is completely sand free. They promise fast, dust free results that are the same or better than traditional hardwood floor refinishing. For one thing, these systems have been around for a long time and are used by most if not all traditional, professional floor refinishers. Basically it involves using a type of chemical cleaner and a buffer to abrade the top coat of an existing floor and then a new top coat of finish is applied. Some guys have found it to be faster, easier money as they don't have to invest in expensive proper tools and equipment to do a complete restoration. And the truth is in some cases it can be a great option. But... (and this is a big but) your floors have to be in very good shape originally for this to be a viable option. If you have dings, scratches, bruises, stains, greyed wear through areas or any other damage to the actual wood - not just the finish - then no matter how many recoats you do, they will not be removed. That's what a lot of these guys "forget" to tell you. If you just recoat over damage, you'll just have a nice shiny new top coat that highlights the damaged parts even more. A total waste of time and money. If this really worked as some guys promise, why wouldn't we be offering it and only specialize in 'dustless, no-sanding recoats' too? It would be much easier on us and we'd definitely make more money. The truth is, the only way to remove this type of wear and damage is to completely remove the existing finish, take the floor back to bear wood, repair the damaged areas and then apply a whole new finish system. Then you'll have floors that are even better than new because of the latest finishes we can apply. On the other hand, if your hardwood floor have been restored by us previously and you have followed our cleaning and maintenance instructions, then a clean and recoat could most certainly be an option. (This assumes that the finish is not totally worn through and you are not worried about removing most of the surface scratches.) In fact recoating your floors every 5 to 10 years is highly recommended. If you have a chance to do them before the finish wears through, it will most certainly prolong the life of your hardwood and reduce the need for re-sanding them down the road. Just to give you the heads up - we won't recoat floors that we haven't previously restored. This is because out of all the hardwood restoration processes, recoats have the biggest failure rates. And it's always the same thing that causes it - the homeowner hasn't followed good care instructions and now waxes, oils or some other type of chemical has been applied or spilled on the floor which now prevents the new finish from adhering. Even the "recoat specialists" have high failure rates that you can read about on many forums all over the internet. Yes, doing it properly and right will cost a little more and take a bit longer, but we feel the exceptionally better end results are worth it.

Hardwood Flooring Installation

Installation methods vary and depend on multiple factors including type of subfloor location, the level of the house where it is being installed, and the structure of the hardwood being used.

Prefinished and Unfinished Wood

Nail-down only:

Each plank is directly nailed to the subfloor. This method of installation requires a inch plywood subfloor and is the required minimum for all solid wood flooring.

Adhesive only (no nails used):

Each plank is glued directly onto the subfloor. This installation is recommended only for engineered wood, not solid wood, because engineered wood is more stable than solid wood. There are adhesive manufacturers who say you can glue down solid. However, we have seen this method fail and therefore we do not recommend it.

Combination nail-down and adhesive:

Wood is responsive to the climate and weather; it expands in humidity and contracts in dry heat. The wider the wood planks, the stronger this effect is. Glue can be used in installation as an additional measure to hold the floor in place when the wood expands and contracts during the change of seasons and subsequent humidity levels.

Floating:

Planks of flooring are attached by interlocking the tongues and grooves of the wood, or glued to each other, but are not attached directly to the subfloor. As the floor contracts and expands with the temperature and humidity, it moves as one unit. This application is only used for engineered wood because engineered wood is more stable.

Hardwood Repairs

If installed by skilled floor masters, floors should be very durable. However, even the best floors experience wear and tear over the years. Our team of expert carpenters and craftsmen are key in rebuilding your floor as close to its original beauty as possible.

We offer two types of flooring improvement services.

Repairs:

Sometimes, though the general condition of flooring is good, there are small areas that need to be repaired. For example, water-damaged areas, broken or loosened boards, or areas missing wood due to the redesign of wall layout. We also correct problematic repairs performed by unskilled technicians. he maintenance coat is clear, there is no need to move large furniture away from the area being treated. This is not a spot repair; the entire area must be treated in order for it to blend in.

Restoration:

Restoration: Vintage hardwood flooring often includes unique patterns, inlays, or medallions that are damaged over time. To restore the floor to its original beauty, these missing or damaged pieces need to be custom cut and filled in.

Hardwood Staining

After the wood is sanded, the stain is applied. Staining is what gives the wood color. Stains and finishes can be combined to create unique colors.

We provide an additional service of sampling stains on the wood. A technician will come to your home and apply 10 stain color samples to your wood floor prior to scheduling the sanding of your floor. We do this because wood is natural and the stain may look different on your floor than it does on another piece of wood. Why 10? Stains come in 5 basic colors black, white, brown, red, and gold. By mixing them we can achieve more options. We will mix and change the mixture to give you many options and allow you to choose the exact color that you prefer. You will have time to consider the stain samples in various lighting before making a decision. There is no pressure to choose a color right away.

To ensure the richest, deepest colors for your wood floor, we may use a process called water-popping to open the wood pores in your floor.

We offer several types of stains to achieve the color and richness you desire for your floor.

MinWax and Duraseal:

These stains can be used with standard polyurethane finishes and can be mixed to achieve a wide variety of color options. Multiple layers of polyurethane are applied on top of the stain.

Rubio Monocoat:

Rubio Monocoat is an ecologically friendly, penetrating finish made from natural plant oils. It does not contain any VOCs (volatile organic compounds) nor does it give off a bad odor. It is applied in a single coat, which includes both color and finish. Rubio Monocoat molecularly bonds to the wood so it will not peel or flake off, and it can be spot repaired. It is available in 30 colors.

Rubio Monocoat Fume:

Fume is a pre-staining treatment often applied to the floor with a brush. This special product is an addition to the staining process, which enriches color by turning the wood a slight grey color. Not all stains work with this fuming process. Test various stains to ensure compatibility.

WOCA:

WOCA oils are also a penetrating finish. They contain primarily cold-pressed vegetable oils and aromatic hardening oils, also called urethane. As the wood cures, these oils harden to form a very protective surface that becomes part of the wood floor. Small scratches and marks do not show up as much on a floor with a urethane finish.

Maintenance

The best of floors require upkeep to maintain their beauty and durability. But eventually, all finishes wear out. To avoid the necessity of resanding and a full refinishing, a maintenance coat can help extend the life of the flooring.

What you can expect from your Tribeca Flooring team:

Oil- and water-based polyurethane finishes:

The top layer of polyurethane is lightly sanded off in a process called screening, and a new coat of polyurethane is applied. Because the maintenance coat is clear, there is no need to move large furniture away from the area being treated. This is not a spot repair; the entire area must be treated in order for it to blend in.

Natural oil: The benefit of this finish is that it looks much more natural.

Natural oil wax penetrates wood rather than sitting on top of it like polyurethane. This finish requires more maintenance, which can easily be done by a homeowner or by a technician. The scratches on a natural-oil-finished floor can be easily spot-treated and will match perfectly without having to sand and refinish the entire floor, like one would need to do with a poly-finished floor. In summary: it wears off more quickly but is more easily maintained.

Buffing and Recoating Wood Floors

Most floors have 3 coats of polyurethane. Don't wait until all three coats of polyurethane have been destroyed, or you will have to resand and refinish your entire floor, which can be costly and inconvenient. Avoid resanding and refinishing by periodically maintaining the top layer of polyurethane with a buff and coat. This will result in your floor looking fresh and clean each time you reapply the topcoat of polyurethane. Remember, only the top layer will be freshened, so for an y marks that penetrate deeper than the top layer of polyurethane, a buff and coat is not enough.

Plywood Subfloor Leveling and Installation

A quality floor begins with a quality subfloor as a foundation. There are three methods to choose from for installing subflooring: Sleepers, plywood-on-slab and floating subfloor system.

For a floor that is not yet completely level, we place 66-inch boxes upon the base and then lay the subfloor on top. Finally, the wood floor is installed flush against the level subfloor.

Sleepers

The sleeper system is designed to prevent warping and mold growth, which ultimately extends the life of your floor. S leepers are 2-by-4 lengths of wood on the slab spaced 16 inches on center.

Plywood

One layer of inch plywood or Advantech : The subfloor, comprised of plywood or Advantech, is installed directly onto the existing floor - either a concrete slab or wood joists - and attached with screws for wood subfloor or split drive anchors for concrete subfloor.

Floating subfloor

Double-layer of 4/8" floating plywood or Advantech. The two layers are laid out in opposite directions, and are then glued and screwed together. The subfloor can be floated over foam or rubber insulation. This is the best solution for applying subflooring on top of sound barrier material because it can be done without puncturing it with nails.

This process is used for all three subflooring systems:

1. We build up the low spots with custom 66-inch boxes

2. We lay the plywood on top of the boxes

3. The result: a perfectly level and stable subfloor

The Process of Sanding and Refinishing

Sand:

The sanding process uses progressively finer abrasive material to remove the layers of finish covering the hardwood. A dustless sanding option is available, utilizing specialized machinery with a vacuum suction to collect dust and sand. When the raw wood is exposed, it is ready to be stained with a color and polished, or finished with its natural color.

Varnish:

A final finish that preserves wood and protects it from scratches, and also highlights the beauty of a natural wood floor. There are three kinds of varnishes: polyurethanes-which are either oil-based or water-based-natural oil, and hardwax oil

CAUTION

If done incorrectly, finishing a floor can be dangerous. Here are some of the ways that we ensure your safety and the safety of our team members while we refinish your floors.

Fire Hazards

Many chemicals, especially lacquer sealants, are extremely flammable.

Therefore, we take the following preventive measures:

Eliminate a fire hazard by using a water-based sealant instead of lacquer-based sealants. Water-based solvent-based finishes can be very attractive and less dangerous.

To protect against a flash fire, we make sure that the work area is well-ventilated.

Ensure that there are no flames nearby, such as a pilot light in the kitchen oven.

No smoking on premises! A cigarette can easily catch fire to the solvents.

A fire extinguisher is on-site at all times, just in case.

Spontaneous Combustion

When sanding the floor, if the wood dust and old finish combine they can combust. Also, if rags used to apply stain to the floor are not disposed of properly, they can react with oxygen as they dry, giving off heat. If the heat becomes trapped in one area without enough ventilation the rags can begin to smolder and burn even without a fire to light it. Additionally, heat created from the friction of the sandpaper machine on the floor can cause the wood dust and old finish to smolder inside the machine's bag. Even if the machine's bag is emptied often, there is still risk of smoldering.

Therefore, we follow these preventive measures:

No stain-soaked rags or garbage bags of wood dust are left inside your home.

We offer an optional dustless system so there is less risk of dust mixing with other chemicals or flammable sources.

Electrical Dangers

Specific power hookups are required for the machines used in floor sanding. When we come to your home to discuss your flooring project with you, we will look for a proper 220V hookup to ensure safety for you and our team.

Be sure to have the following in place before our crew arrives:

A proper 220V hookup.

A 30amp breaker.

A Final Word

By hiring a highly skilled team, you are taking preventive measures against the dangers associated with floor finishing. If safety is as important to you as it is to us, you will understand the extra measures that we take to keep you, and ourselves, safe.

Hardwood Flooring Installation

The flooring of every space varies slightly from one to the next. During the initial consultation -- either by phone or at the job site -- we conduct an in-depth analysis of the flooring project, listen to your expectations, and explain all of the available options for achieving your goals.

At Tribeca Flooring we use the following quality flooring products for installation. These materials are also available for purchase. We have the resources and expertise to meet any project scope or budget.

Prefinished Wood

Hardwood flooring that is sanded, stained, and finished in the factory

Advantages:

The factory-applied wood finish is very durable. The more coats applied, the more protected the floor is. Factories can apply up to 14 coats, resulting in a stronger finish, which protects the floor against wear and tear.

These planks are manufactured with micro bevels - rounded edges to help the sharp corners of the wooden planks align perfectly. This method is used in place of sanding the floors, which would eliminate any alignment issues.

Wood samples depict exactly how the completed flooring project will look.

Repairs are easy, and replacement pieces of wood can be procured from the same collection to match flooring perfectly.

Many more finishing options are available. Finishing plants are not as limited in their capabilities as job sites are, so the plants are able to manipulate a multitude of finishes in their controlled environment.

No mess is left behind because no sanding is required.

The overall project time is shorter.

Because this wood is already finished, there is no need to move furniture out of your home while the floors are sanded and finished. We can move your furniture as needed.

No lag time; the floor is ready to be walked upon the day it is installed.

Prefinished wood can be re-sanded and refinished just like unfinished wood.

There are less fumes with a pre-finished wood floor.

There are better warranties with pre-finished floors.

Disadvantages

The micro bevels around the edges of the wood planks collect dust and can be more challenging to clean than smooth floors. (There is one company that offers prefinished wood without micro bevels. This product can only be used if your subfloor is perfectly straight. Otherwise, sharp edges may result.)

Complex patterns are more difficult to achieve with pre-finished wood.

Unfinished Wood

Traditional raw, hardwood flooring that is installed raw, and then sanded, stained, and finished on site.

The sanding process creates a completely flat and smooth floor (no bevels, no grooves), resulting in a floor that appears as one whole piece of wood.

Easy to clean because there are no grooves to trap dirt.

Freedom to customize these floors to your heart's desire - any grade, size, or species of wood, any color of stain, inlays, borders, medallions, patterns, and levels of sheen.

It is more difficult to repair unfinished wood because the finish was applied as one coat to the entire floor as a whole. In order for the floor to appear uniform, the whole floor must be refinished.

Additional time is needed for sanding and finishing the floors.

Each piece of unfinished wood is unique, so the completed floor will not match the flooring sample exactly. The stain sample does not take into account the various types of wood that you might have used throughout the flooring project area. Additionally, the stain sample does not take into account the polyurethane coats that will be applied on top of it.

The sanding process produces a lot of dust. For an additional fee, we offer an effective dustless process that will remove the dust while the floors are being installed.

Odor varies by finish type. Some finishes smell awful and some finishes hardly have any smell at all.

How Do You Choose?

Consider: Can you clear your entire jobsite for it to be sanded? Do you want a unique, custom floor with specific design and color? Do you mind bevels in your floor? Do you have the necessary time to finish the floors on-site?

The choice is yours. We will provide you with unmatched service, regardless of your choice of flooring. The final project cost depends on your unique needs and circumstances.

There are two types of wood structures, solid and engineered, which are both available in unfinished and factory-finished wood:

Solid wood

Structure:

Traditional hardwood flooring that is comprised of a solid wood plank, generally inches thick.

Availability:

These generally come in 2-inch-wide to 7-inch-wide planks and can be custom-made up to 12-inches wide.

Environment:

Wood naturally expands and contracts with moisture, so it is important to maintain a steady level of humidity in the house.

Application Method:

This flooring must be adhered to a wood subfloor using nail or staples. It is recommended to glue the floor down as well, making the floor even stronger. This holds the floor in place when moisture levels fluctuate in the home.

Benefit:

A great benefit of solid hardwood is that is can be sanded and refinished many, many times.

Engineered wood

Flooring comprised of two products, solid wood veneer (wear) layer and engineered wood layer. The engineered layers of wood have grains that go in opposite directions from the grains of the solid wood that is above it, allowing for the layers to support one another and resist natural expanding and contracting.

The engineered layer generally comes in -inch-thick to -inch-thick planks. The veneer layer above it generally comes 1-millimeter-thick to 6-millimeter-thick. Because it is a stronger product, wider planks are more common.

While humidity levels in the environment cause a solid wood floor to expand and contract, engineered wood has the benefit of being more stable and more resistant to those changes in humidity levels. Although engineered wood is more stable than solid wood, no wood is completely resistant to changes in humidity levels in the environment.

Consider your priorities: What kind of subfloor do you currently have? What solution is the best fit for your environment? How wide of a plank would you like in your flooring?

The following materials are used beneath the wood flooring as an underlay:

Subflooring wood

Base of the flooring laid on top of your existing concrete foundation. It is used to support the wood flooring to be laid above it, which can be nailed down into the subfloor. Manufactured as plywood or Advantech [link to website]. Alternatively, a joist slab of wood can also be used.

Subflooring wood is used for the following reasons:

To nail a wood floor to it (a wood subfloor is required to nail into)

To level the floor before installing any type of floor-applied with nails, glue, or by floating the floor above it.

Sound-reduction products

Underlayments to reduce traveling noise. There are multiple options for sound-reduction such as rubber underlayment, foam underlayment, or 3-in-one glue, which works as a moisture and sound reduction barrier in addition to the adhesive.

Installation methods vary and depend on multiple factors including type of subfloor location, the level of the house where it is being installed, and the structure of the hardwood being used.

Prefinished and Unfinished Wood

Nail-down only:

Each plank is directly nailed to the subfloor. This method of installation requires a inch plywood subfloor and is the required minimum for all solid wood flooring.

Adhesive only (no nails used):

Each plank is glued directly onto the subfloor. This installation is recommended only for engineered wood, not solid wood, because engineered wood is more stable than solid wood. There are adhesive manufacturers who say you can glue down solid. However, we have seen this method fail and therefore we do not recommend it.

Combination nail-down and adhesive:

Wood is responsive to the climate and weather; it expands in humidity and contracts in dry heat. The wider the wood planks, the stronger this effect is. Glue can be used in installation as an additional measure to hold the floor in place when the wood expands and contracts during the change of seasons and subsequent humidity levels.

Floating:

Planks of flooring are attached by interlocking the tongues and grooves of the wood, or glued to each other, but are not attached directly to the subfloor. As the floor contracts and expands with the temperature and humidity, it moves as one unit. This application is only used for engineered wood because engineered wood is more stable.

Sound-Reduction Installation

A quality floor begins with quality Sound-Reduction.

There are three methods to choose from for installing the Sound-Reduction.

Foam underlayment:

This product is unrolled on top of the subfloor and the new floor is installed with a floating method on top of it. This product can only be installed as a floating underlayment; it cannot be nailed or glued down, because the foam can tear.

Rubber underlayment:

This is a rubber product made of recycled materials, used between concrete and plywood or concrete and engineered wood. It offers great sound resistance and is versatile and resilient. When using engineered wood on top of the rubber underlayment, the materials should be glued down or floated. this can be used either for glue down engineered wood, floating engineered wood, floating plywood subfloor installation or anchoring down plywood

Three-in-one adhesive:

This material functions as an adhesive and as a barrier to moisture and sound.

Step-By-Step Guide to Our Sound-Reduction Installation

Foam underlayment : This material is only used for a floating floor installation. The foam is unrolled on top of the subfloor and the new floor is installed with a floating method on top of it. The underlayment is not glued because if you glue it, the foam will rip.

Rubber underlayment: Rubber can be used either for glued-down engineered wood, floating engineered wood, floating plywood subfloor installation, or anchoring down plywood.

Engineered Wood installation on top of rubber underlayment: The rubber is either glued down or laid out on top of the existing subfloor, and then a engineered floor can be glued to or floated on top of the rubber.

Subfloor installation on top of rubber: There are two methods for installing subfloor on top of rubber underlayment.

Anchoring: Rubber is laid out on top of the existing floor. Then one layer of plywood is anchored through the rubber and into the floor. (By doing it this way you are losing some of the effectiveness because the anchors will be puncturing the rubber allowing sound to travel through the anchors.)

Floating: Rubber is laid out and then a double layer of plywood is floated on top of the rubber. This creates a floating subfloor.

3 in 1 glue: This is a wood flooring adhesive that also works as a sound and moisture reducer. It can be used whenever a solid or engineered floor is being glued down.

Wood samples depict exactly how the completed flooring project will look.

Repairs are easy, and replacement pieces of wood can be procured from the same collection to match flooring perfectly.

Many more finishing options are available. Finishing plants are not as limited in their capabilities as job sites are, so the plants are able to manipulate a multitude of finishes in their controlled environment.

No mess is left behind because no sanding is required.

The overall project time is shorter.

Because this wood is already finished, there is no need to move furniture out of your home while the floors are sanded and finished. We can move your furniture as needed.

No lag time; the floor is ready to be walked upon the day it is installed.

Prefinished wood can be re-sanded and refinished just like unfinished wood.

There are less fumes with a pre-finished wood floor.

There are better warranties with pre-finished floors.

Disadvantages

The micro bevels around the edges of the wood planks collect dust and can be more challenging to clean than smooth floors. (There is one company that offers prefinished wood without micro bevels. This product can only be used if your subfloor is perfectly straight. Otherwise, sharp edges may result.)

Complex patterns are more difficult to achieve with pre-finished wood.

Unfinished Wood

Traditional raw, hardwood flooring that is installed raw, and then sanded, stained, and finished on site.

The sanding process creates a completely flat and smooth floor (no bevels, no grooves), resulting in a floor that appears as one whole piece of wood.

Easy to clean because there are no grooves to trap dirt.

Freedom to customize these floors to your heart's desire - any grade, size, or species of wood, any color of stain, inlays, borders, medallions, patterns, and levels of sheen.

It is more difficult to repair unfinished wood because the finish was applied as one coat to the entire floor as a whole. In order for the floor to appear uniform, the whole floor must be refinished.

Additional time is needed for sanding and finishing the floors.

Each piece of unfinished wood is unique, so the completed floor will not match the flooring sample exactly. The stain sample does not take into account the various types of wood that you might have used throughout the flooring project area. Additionally, the stain sample does not take into account the polyurethane coats that will be applied on top of it.

The sanding process produces a lot of dust. For an additional fee, we offer an effective dustless process that will remove the dust while the floors are being installed.

Odor varies by finish type. Some finishes smell awful and some finishes hardly have any smell at all.

How Do You Choose?

Consider: Can you clear your entire jobsite for it to be sanded? Do you want a unique, custom floor with specific design and color? Do you mind bevels in your floor? Do you have the necessary time to finish the floors on-site?

The choice is yours. We will provide you with unmatched service, regardless of your choice of flooring. The final project cost depends on your unique needs and circumstances.

There are two types of wood structures, solid and engineered, which are both available in unfinished and factory-finished wood:

Solid wood

Structure:

Traditional hardwood flooring that is comprised of a solid wood plank, generally inches thick.

Availability:

These generally come in 2-inch-wide to 7-inch-wide planks and can be custom-made up to 12-inches wide.

Environment:

Wood naturally expands and contracts with moisture, so it is important to maintain a steady level of humidity in the house.

Application Method:

This flooring must be adhered to a wood subfloor using nail or staples. It is recommended to glue the floor down as well, making the floor even stronger. This holds the floor in place when moisture levels fluctuate in the home.

Benefit:

A great benefit of solid hardwood is that is can be sanded and refinished many, many times.

Engineered wood

Flooring comprised of two products, solid wood veneer (wear) layer and engineered wood layer. The engineered layers of wood have grains that go in opposite directions from the grains of the solid wood that is above it, allowing for the layers to support one another and resist natural expanding and contracting.

The engineered layer generally comes in -inch-thick to -inch-thick planks. The veneer layer above it generally comes 1-millimeter-thick to 6-millimeter-thick. Because it is a stronger product, wider planks are more common.

While humidity levels in the environment cause a solid wood floor to expand and contract, engineered wood has the benefit of being more stable and more resistant to those changes in humidity levels. Although engineered wood is more stable than solid wood, no wood is completely resistant to changes in humidity levels in the environment.

Consider your priorities: What kind of subfloor do you currently have? What solution is the best fit for your environment? How wide of a plank would you like in your flooring?

The following materials are used beneath the wood flooring as an underlay:

Subflooring wood

Base of the flooring laid on top of your existing concrete foundation. It is used to support the wood flooring to be laid above it, which can be nailed down into the subfloor. Manufactured as plywood or Advantech [link to website]. Alternatively, a joist slab of wood can also be used.

Subflooring wood is used for the following reasons:

To nail a wood floor to it (a wood subfloor is required to nail into)

To level the floor before installing any type of floor-applied with nails, glue, or by floating the floor above it.

Sound-reduction products

Underlayments to reduce traveling noise. There are multiple options for sound-reduction such as rubber underlayment, foam underlayment, or 3-in-one glue, which works as a moisture and sound reduction barrier in addition to the adhesive.
