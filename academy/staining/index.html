Lesson 7

Staining and color

Staining gives wood color. Sampling gives the client confidence before the whole floor changes.

Sample before committing

Wood is natural, so stain may look different on one floor than it does on another piece of wood. Apply samples directly on the project floor and view them in the room's lighting before choosing.

Tribeca's original process sampled ten stain colors because stains can begin with black, white, brown, red, and gold, then be mixed into many options.

Color systems

MinWax and Duraseal

These stains can be used with standard polyurethane finishes and mixed to achieve many colors. Multiple polyurethane coats are applied above the stain.

Rubio Monocoat

A penetrating plant-oil finish with color and finish in one coat. It bonds to wood, does not peel or flake, and can be spot repaired.

Rubio Monocoat Fume

A pre-staining treatment that can enrich color by turning wood slightly gray. Test compatibility before committing.

WOCA oils

Penetrating oils that harden into a protective surface. Small scratches and marks are often less visible than with film finishes.

Water-popping

Water-popping opens the wood pores before stain. It can deepen color, but it also increases the need for even application and careful sample approval.
