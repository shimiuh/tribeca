Sound-Reduction Installation

A quality floor begins with quality Sound-Reduction.

There are three methods to choose from for installing the Sound-Reduction.
Foam underlayment:

This product is unrolled on top of the subfloor and the new floor is installed with a floating method on top of it. This product can only be installed as a floating underlayment; it cannot be nailed or glued down, because the foam can tear.

 

Rubber underlayment:

This is a rubber product made of recycled materials, used between concrete and plywood or concrete and engineered wood. It offers great sound resistance and is versatile and resilient. When using engineered wood on top of the rubber underlayment, the materials should be glued down or floated. this can be used either for glue down engineered wood, floating engineered wood, floating plywood subfloor installation or anchoring down plywood

Three-in-one adhesive:

This material functions as an adhesive and as a barrier to moisture and sound.

Step-By-Step Guide to Our Sound-Reduction Installation
  1. Foam underlayment: This material is only used for a floating floor installation. The foam is unrolled on top of the subfloor and the new floor is installed with a floating method on top of it. The underlayment is not glued because if you glue it, the foam will rip.
  2. Rubber underlayment: Rubber can be used either for glued-down engineered wood, floating engineered wood, floating plywood subfloor installation, or anchoring down plywood.
    1. Engineered Wood installation on top of rubber underlayment: The rubber is either glued down or laid out on top of the existing subfloor, and then a engineered floor can be glued to or floated on top of the rubber.
    2. Subfloor installation on top of rubber: There are two methods for installing subfloor on top of rubber underlayment.
      1. Anchoring: Rubber is laid out on top of the existing floor. Then one layer of plywood is anchored through the rubber and into the floor. (By doing it this way you are losing some of the effectiveness because the anchors will be puncturing the rubber allowing sound to travel through the anchors.)
      2. Floating: Rubber is laid out and then a double layer of plywood is floated on top of the rubber. This creates a floating subfloor.
  3. 3 in 1 glue: This is a wood flooring adhesive that also works as a sound and moisture reducer. It can be used whenever a solid or engineered floor is being glued down.
