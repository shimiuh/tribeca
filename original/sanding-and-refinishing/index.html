Sanding and Refinishing—Hardwood Floors and Stairs:

Whether you are installing hardwood flooring for the first time or want to sand down and refinish an existing floor, Tribeca Flooring has the resources and expertise to meet any project scope or budget.

Commercial Crews:

Our standard commercial-construction crews do excellent work and will finish your commercial floors beautifully and cost-effectively. Our crews focus on staying true to the project schedule and budget. They are limited by the stain colors they can use in the flooring.

Residential Crews:

Our expert residential crews utilize precise craftsmanship to create the art of a unique, ornate wood floor. Our crews are true craftsmen in that they work at perfecting their art and take extreme pride in every inch that they sand and stain. For this reason, they take as much time as they need to prep, sand, and finish the floors they are working on. They are trained to use many products and can therefore achieve a wide variety of wood colors and finish options.

The Process of Sanding and Refinishing

Sand:

The sanding process uses progressively finer abrasive material to remove the layers of finish covering the hardwood. A dustless sanding option is available, utilizing specialized machinery with a vacuum suction to collect dust and sand. When the raw wood is exposed, it is ready to be stained with a color and polished, or finished with its natural color.

Varnish:

A final finish that preserves wood and protects it from scratches, and also highlights the beauty of a natural wood floor. There are three kinds of varnishes: polyurethanes—which are either oil-based or water-based—natural oil, and hardwax oil

Step-By-Step Guide to Our Hardwood Floor Refinishing Process
With our laser-focused attention to detail we guarantee a beautifully finished wood floor that you will enjoy for years to come.
  1. Seal off areas to protect against dust (optional)
  2. Set up dustless equipment (optional)
  3. Sweep dirt off of the floor
  4. Vacuum floor to remove dust
  5. Inspect floor for protruding splinters or nails
  6. Sand to remove existing finish
    1. The floor is sanded with a machine with a round belt, called an edger, which sands the floor in a circular motion.
    2. The number of times we sand your floor depends on the condition of the floor and the type of finish it has. When we sand, we try to remove as little wood as possible to ensure the longevity of your wood flooring.
  7. Vacuum to remove dust
  8. Final sanding with soft sandpaper
  9. Hand-scrape and sand the edges and corners of the area
  10. Vacuum to remove dust
  11. Buff and flatten floor
  12. Stain floor – if desired. See more information on staining here. [link]
  13. Inspect the stain
  14. Analyze the flooring for perfection
  15. Vacuum
  16. Apply first coat of wood finish
    1. If using a Rubio Monocoat, finish, this is the final step.
  17. Buff the floor using a light sanding process to roughen the surface slightly so the next layer of poly will grip it well and not peel off.
  18. Vacuum to remove dust
  19. Tack floor – use a dry lint-free rag to pick up any remaining sand or dust
  20. If applying another coat of finish, buff the floor once again to prep it for the finish.
  21. Apply second coat of wood finish
  22. Vacuum to remove dust
  23. Tack floor to remove remaining dust and sand
  24. Apply third and final coat of wood finish
  25. Final walk-through to ensure perfection
More About Hardwood Floor Refinishing Process
CAUTION

If done incorrectly, finishing a floor can be dangerous. Here are some of the ways that we ensure your safety and the safety of our team members while we refinish your floors.

Fire Hazards

Many chemicals, especially lacquer sealants, are extremely flammable.

Therefore, we take the following preventive measures:

  1. Eliminate a fire hazard by using a water-based sealant instead of lacquer-based sealants. Water-based solvent-based finishes can be very attractive and less dangerous.
  2. To protect against a flash fire, we make sure that the work area is well-ventilated.
  3. Ensure that there are no flames nearby, such as a pilot light in the kitchen oven.
  4. No smoking on premises! A cigarette can easily catch fire to the solvents.
  5. A fire extinguisher is on-site at all times, just in case.

Spontaneous Combustion

When sanding the floor, if the wood dust and old finish combine they can combust. Also, if rags used to apply stain to the floor are not disposed of properly, they can react with oxygen as they dry, giving off heat. If the heat becomes trapped in one area without enough ventilation the rags can begin to smolder and burn even without a fire to light it. Additionally, heat created from the friction of the sandpaper machine on the floor can cause the wood dust and old finish to smolder inside the machine’s bag. Even if the machine’s bag is emptied often, there is still risk of smoldering.

Therefore, we follow these preventive measures:

  1. No stain-soaked rags or garbage bags of wood dust are left inside your home.
  2. We offer an optional dustless system so there is less risk of dust mixing with other chemicals or flammable sources.

Electrical Dangers

Specific power hookups are required for the machines used in floor sanding. When we come to your home to discuss your flooring project with you, we will look for a proper 220V hookup to ensure safety for you and our team.

Be sure to have the following in place before our crew arrives:

  1. A proper 220V hookup.
  2. A 30amp breaker.

A Final Word

By hiring a highly skilled team, you are taking preventive measures against the dangers associated with floor finishing. If safety is as important to you as it is to us, you will understand the extra measures that we take to keep you, and ourselves, safe.
